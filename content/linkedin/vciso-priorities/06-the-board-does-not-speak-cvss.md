Matthew Sharp and Kyriakos Lambros, both practicing security executives, wrote *The CISO Evolution* (Wiley, 2022) to solve a specific problem. Security leaders keep getting cut from budget conversations because they cannot translate technical risk into business terms. Their argument: cybersecurity must be treated as enterprise risk within a COSO ERM framework, not as an IT problem with a specialized vocabulary.

Barak Engel wrote *Why CISOs Fail* (CRC Press, 2017) after decades as a CISO. He calls the security leader the "Department of How," not the "Department of No." The failure modes he catalogs, the compliance trap, the technology obsession, are all the same failure. The CISO who speaks in CVSS scores to a board that allocates capital in dollars and probabilities has not communicated.

Sharp and Lambros provide the practical toolkit. Storytelling outperforms data presentation in executive settings. In one cited experiment, 63 percent of listeners recalled a story versus 5 percent who recalled a statistic. The Socratic method of questioning exposes contradictions in stakeholders' reasoning without direct confrontation.

Engel's best operational example: do not tell the business they cannot launch without a security review. Ask what the feature does for customers. Explain the risks in terms of the business outcome. Propose controls that enable the launch while managing the risk. The business hears "how we can do this safely" instead of "no."

For a security leader, this is the difference between getting budget and getting ignored. The person who can translate a technical finding into a business case gets funded. The person who cannot gets invited to fewer meetings. If you cannot express a risk in terms the CFO uses for other decisions, you have not communicated anything. You have performed a ritual the business has learned to tolerate.
