The board needs probabilities and dollar ranges. Not red, yellow, and green.

Sharp and Lambros, in *The CISO Evolution*, make the case directly: cybersecurity must be treated as enterprise risk rather than IT risk. COSO ERM provides the organizing structure. The portfolio view of risk, aggregating cyber risks with other enterprise risks for board-level prioritization, is the mechanism.

But the translation problem is real. Engel calls the CISO the "Department of How," not the "Department of No." The failure modes he catalogs, the compliance trap, the technology obsession, the dismal discipline, are all variants of the same failure. The CISO who speaks in technical severity ratings to a board that thinks in financial terms has not communicated.

Sharp and Lambros provide the practical toolkit. Storytelling is more effective than data presentation for persuading executives. In one cited experiment, 63 percent of listeners recalled a story versus 5 percent who recalled a statistic. The Socratic method of questioning exposes contradictions in stakeholders' reasoning without direct confrontation.

Here is Engel at his best. Do not tell the business they cannot launch the new feature without a security review. Ask what the feature does for customers. Explain the risks in terms of the business outcome. Propose controls that enable the launch while managing the risk. The business hears "how we can do this safely" instead of "no."

For the vCISO, this converges on a simple principle. If you cannot express a risk in terms the CFO uses to make other decisions, you have not communicated the risk. You have performed the ritual of communication without the substance. The board calls it a waste of time. The graph calls it the illusion of communication. Same thing.
