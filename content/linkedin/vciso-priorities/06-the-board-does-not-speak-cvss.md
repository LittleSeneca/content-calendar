Sharp and Lambros, in *The CISO Evolution*, argue that cybersecurity must be treated as enterprise risk rather than IT risk. COSO ERM provides the structure. The portfolio view of risk, aggregating cyber risks with other enterprise risks for board-level prioritization, is the mechanism.

Engel calls the CISO the "Department of How," not the "Department of No." The failure modes he catalogs, the compliance trap, the technology obsession, are all the same failure. The CISO who speaks in technical severity ratings to a board that thinks in financial terms has not communicated.

Sharp and Lambros provide the practical side. Storytelling outperforms data presentation in executive settings. In one cited experiment, 63 percent of listeners recalled a story versus 5 percent who recalled a statistic. The Socratic method of questioning exposes contradictions in stakeholders' reasoning without direct confrontation.

Engel's best example: do not tell the business they cannot launch without a security review. Ask what the feature does for customers. Explain the risks in terms of the business outcome. Propose controls that enable the launch while managing the risk. The business hears "how we can do this safely" instead of "no."

If you cannot express a risk in terms the CFO uses to make other decisions, you have not communicated the risk.
